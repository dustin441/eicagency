# Creative Builder concept research

## Product decision

The next Creative Builder iteration starts from one exact landing-page URL. It scans that page, proposes an editable media plan and funnel/audience assumptions, waits for confirmation, generates six materially distinct ad concepts, supports conversational revision, and exports finalized assets. Publishing to ad platforms is explicitly out of scope.

Primary production scope:

- Exact landing page first, homepage only as a bounded brand fallback.
- Meta static placements first: 1080x1080, 1080x1350, and 1080x1920 where the selected format supports it.
- Google Display and Performance Max image assets follow after the Meta workflow is trustworthy.
- Six distinct concepts first, then A/B iterations of the selected two or three concepts.
- Website and user-supplied assets are preferred. AI imagery is optional, not assumed.

## Existing EIC concept source

Parent task: [BOF Native-Style Creative Concepts | $995 White-Label Offer](https://app.clickup.com/t/86bc7h74y)

The task is a direct child of [v2 Ad Creative Tool](https://app.clickup.com/t/86b8aca5q) and contains 11 concept subtasks:

1. **$0 Due Today Receipt**: offer and risk-reversal presented as a receipt.
2. **Agency Owner Text Thread**: a native message exchange that dramatizes the problem and answer.
3. **“This Sounds Fake” Founder Reel**: candid founder explanation and objection handling.
4. **Founder Note**: personal Notes-app or handwritten explanation.
5. **Paid Media Launch Tracker**: process clarity using a familiar order-tracking metaphor.
6. **Breaking-News Editorial**: trade-publication headline treatment.
7. **The Job Posting That Gets Cancelled**: replacement or alternative framed as a native job post.
8. **Agency-Owner Comment Section**: objection-led questions and answers.
9. **One-Client Math**: illustrative calculator, spreadsheet, or napkin math.
10. **What Agencies Think vs. Reality**: expectation-versus-reality comparison.
11. **The White-Label Communication Gap**: proof and objection handling around visibility, updates, and client trust.

The parent task explicitly warns against fabricated reviews, comments, client results, or testimonials. It requires real EIC people/assets/proof where available and keeps internal labels such as BOF out of customer-facing artwork.

## Funnel interpretation

The approved EIC framework is:

- **TOF**: identify the audience, lead with the problem, and make the solution category understandable immediately.
- **MOF**: educate, demonstrate, and build trust for people who know the problem or have engaged.
- **BOF**: clarify the offer, answer objections, provide proof, reduce commitment uncertainty, and make the next step explicit.

Format alone does not determine funnel stage. A founder video can introduce a problem at TOF, explain a process at MOF, or answer a buying objection at BOF.

## External evidence and platform guidance

### True concept diversity, not superficial variants

Meta distinguishes creative diversification from creative iteration. Different CTA text on the same visual is iteration. Distinct audiences, use cases, storylines, messages, and formats create real diversification. Meta recommends a broad portfolio of assets that differ in look, feel, storyline, and message.

Source: [Meta, Demystifying Creative Diversification](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/demystifying-creative-diversification)

Product implication: the first six outputs must represent six different persuasion mechanisms, not six visual reskins of one headline.

### Native and placement-specific execution

Meta describes native ads as paid content that mirrors the surrounding environment. Its creative guidance recommends vertical video, early message clarity, safe zones, recognizable people, product or service focus, high-resolution imagery, clear branding, thoughtful text, and a mix of image and video.

Sources:

- [Meta Creative Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/inspiration)
- [Meta, Native advertising](https://www.facebook.com/business/resources/native-advertising)

Product implication: a receipt, text thread, Notes screenshot, comment thread, editorial card, founder video, and tracker should retain their native visual grammar instead of being forced into one generic branded template.

### Copy variants and explicit CTA

Meta recommends short copy, one clear action, placement-specific copy, and multiple primary-text/headline options when testing. It notes that most placements recommend approximately 125 characters for primary text, 40 for headline, and 25 for description, although truncation varies.

Source: [Meta, Creative best practices for text in ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/223409425500940)

Product implication: each selected concept should be able to produce bounded A/B copy variants while preserving a single strategic angle.

### Creative matters, but no concept is guaranteed to convert

Nielsen reports that creative remains a leading sales driver, while targeting, reach, recency, and context also materially affect outcomes. Its cited methodology concerns FMCG campaigns, so it cannot establish that a specific EIC or B2B concept will win.

Source: [Nielsen, When it Comes to Advertising Effectiveness, What is Key?](https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2017/when-it-comes-to-advertising-effectiveness-what-is-key)

Product implication: label outputs as test hypotheses and recommended concepts, never predicted winners or guaranteed high-converting ads.

## Reusable concept taxonomy

The generator should select from persuasion jobs and then choose a suitable visual treatment.

| Persuasion job | Example treatments | Typical funnel use | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem recognition | bold problem statement, editorial headline, founder hook | TOF | page language or user-confirmed problem |
| Category/solution explanation | process tracker, annotated workflow, visual metaphor | TOF/MOF | confirmed service/process description |
| Demonstration | product/process capture, before/after workflow, screen recording | MOF | approved real artifact |
| Human trust | founder note, founder reel, expert point of view | MOF | consenting real person or clearly labeled draft |
| Proof | dashboard view, case-study artifact, verified testimonial | MOF/BOF | first-party proof and permission |
| Objection handling | comment Q&A, text thread, FAQ, “sounds fake” explanation | MOF/BOF | real or explicitly illustrative questions |
| Comparison | expectation vs reality, alternatives, side-by-side | MOF/BOF | comparable definitions and substantiated claims |
| Offer clarity | receipt, pricing card, package summary | BOF | confirmed price, scope, terms, and CTA |
| Economic case | calculator, one-client math, cost-of-delay | BOF | verified inputs or prominent illustrative labeling |
| Low-friction process | launch tracker, timeline, checklist | BOF | confirmed milestones and constraints |
| Social/native conversation | message thread, comment feed, post screenshot | all stages | never impersonate or fabricate a real person |
| Pattern interruption | job post, memo, newspaper, teardown, annotated artifact | all stages | format-specific disclosure and truthful copy |

## Recommended six-concept portfolio rule

The six default concepts should normally cover six different jobs:

1. Problem recognition or category hook.
2. Human/founder point of view.
3. Product, service, or process demonstration.
4. Proof or operational credibility.
5. Objection handling or comparison.
6. Offer, economics, or next-step clarity.

The exact visual treatments should vary based on the page. For the EIC white-label offer, a strong initial set is:

1. Breaking-News Editorial.
2. Founder Note or Founder Reel.
3. Paid Media Launch Tracker.
4. White-Label Communication Gap with real dashboard proof.
5. Agency Owner Text Thread or Comment Section.
6. $0 Due Today Receipt or One-Client Math.

Do not include both near-duplicates in the first six unless the media plan identifies a distinct audience or funnel job for each.

## Claim and provenance rules

- Page text is a source receipt, not independent verification.
- Extracted claims must retain source excerpts and the exact URL.
- Prices, percentages, guarantees, testimonials, comparisons, and performance claims require explicit confirmation before final export.
- Generated social comments and conversations must be labeled as illustrative unless based on an approved real exchange.
- Generated “news” treatments must not imply earned-media coverage.
- Suggested audience, stage, channel, and concepts are assumptions for human confirmation, not observed performance truth.
- The system may recommend a test, but must not label a concept a winner until first-party campaign results support that claim.
